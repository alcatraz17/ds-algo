# **Trailing Zeros in Factorial**

## *Introduction*
Trailing zeros are the zeros at the end of a number. For example, 5! = 120 has 1 trailing zero, and 10! = 3628800 has 2. Computing n! directly overflows very quickly, so we need a math trick.

## Why the Trick Works
A trailing zero is made by a pair of factors 2 × 5 = 10. In n!, factors of 2 are always more frequent than factors of 5, so we only need to **count the factors of 5** in all numbers from 1 to n.

- Every multiple of 5 contributes at least one 5.
- Every multiple of 25 contributes one extra 5 (25 = 5 × 5).
- Every multiple of 125 contributes one more, and so on.

So the answer is: `floor(n/5) + floor(n/25) + floor(n/125) + ...`

## Efficient Solution
See `04.trailingZeroesOfFactorial.js`.

```JavaScript
const countTrailingZeroes = (n) => {
  let res = 0;
  for (let i = 5; i <= n; i = i * 5) {
    res = res + Math.floor(n / i);
  }
  return res;
};
```

- **Time Complexity**: O(log5(n)) — the loop runs once per power of 5.
- **Space Complexity**: O(1).

## *Key Points to Remember*
1. **Never compute the factorial itself** — it overflows for large n.
2. **Count 5s, not 10s** — there are always enough 2s to pair with them.
3. **Divide by increasing powers of 5** (5, 25, 125, ...) until the power exceeds n.

# **Conclusion**
Trailing zeros in n! are counted by summing floor(n/5) + floor(n/25) + floor(n/125) + ..., which runs in O(log n) time and constant space. It is a classic example of solving a problem with math instead of brute force.
